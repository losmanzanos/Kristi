Therapy

Individual counseling
for adults

Ongoing, conversational, and paced to you rather than to a protocol.

What people bring

Common starting points.

This isn't a menu, and you don't need a diagnosis or a tidy summary to make an appointment. Most people arrive with something closer to "this isn't working and I can't tell you exactly why."

Anxiety & depression

Persistent worry, low mood, the exhausting work of holding it together in public. Both the immediate relief and the pattern underneath it.

Life transitions

Career changes, becoming a parent, the end of a relationship, grief, or moving into a chapter you did not plan for.

Identity & meaning

Questions about faith, purpose, and who you are becoming — especially when the answer has started shifting.

Relationships

Family patterns, conflict that keeps repeating itself, boundaries, and the roles you learned early and never quite put down.

Burnout

Particularly common among helpers, clinicians, clergy, and caregivers — people practiced at attending to everyone else.

Self-knowledge

Work that is less about crisis and more about wanting to understand your own patterns before they choose for you.

The shape of it how it goes

Session by session.

First contact
A short phone call, at no charge, so you can ask questions and get a feel for whether this fits.
First session
Mostly you talking. Some history, some present tense, and a conversation about what you'd want to be different.
Frequency
Weekly to begin with. Some people move to every other week once things settle.
Length
50 minutes.
Format
In person in Littleton, with telehealth available for Colorado residents.
Ending
Deliberately, and with a conversation about it — not by quietly drifting off the schedule.
